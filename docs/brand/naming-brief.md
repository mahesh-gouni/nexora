# Online Business Brand Naming & Build Brief

## 1. Project Overview

This document captures the complete naming discussion and the core
direction for an online business brand. It is intended as a handoff
brief for an AI agent that will continue the work and help build the
brand and its supporting business presence.

### Current concept

The user wants to create an **online business / online shopping brand**.
The exact product category, target audience, business model, and
operating geography have not yet been defined.

The initial naming direction is broad and flexible: a modern, memorable,
professional brand that could work for an online store, e-commerce
business, or potentially a multi-category marketplace.

**Important:** Do not assume the business is definitely a multi-vendor
marketplace or a particular product category. First validate the
business model and scope with the user before making implementation
decisions.

## 2. Naming Goals

The business name should ideally be:

-   Short, memorable, and easy to pronounce.
-   Modern and professional, with a premium feel.
-   Suitable for use as a website, mobile app, social media brand, and
    business identity.
-   Flexible enough to support future business expansion.
-   Distinctive and suitable for branding.
-   Easy to spell and communicate.

Avoid names that are too generic, difficult to spell, or strongly tied
to one product category unless the user confirms a niche.

## 5. Core Brand Idea

Build a trustworthy, easy-to-use online business brand that makes
discovering and accessing products or services convenient.

The brand should communicate:

-   Convenience: customers can access what they need easily.
-   Trust: clear information, dependable service, and a consistent
    experience.
-   Choice: a useful range of products or services, depending on the
    confirmed business model.
-   Simplicity: a clean, intuitive customer experience.
-   Growth: a brand identity that can evolve as the business expands.

Possible broad positioning statement:

> A modern online destination designed to make everyday discovery and
> shopping simpler, more convenient, and more reliable.

This positioning is a starting point, not a confirmed business strategy.

## 6. Work for the AI Agent

Continue from this brief and work with the user to take the idea from
naming exploration to a clearly defined and buildable business.

### Phase 1 --- Understand the business

Before designing or coding, ask the user concise questions to establish:

-   What will the business sell: physical products, digital products,
    services, or a combination?
-   Is it a single online store, a multi-vendor marketplace, a local
    business platform, or another model?
-   Who are the target customers?
-   Which locations or markets will it serve initially?
-   Will the business operate under its own inventory, partner/vendor
    inventory, or both?
-   What makes it different from existing alternatives?
-   Does the user already have a preferred name, domain, logo, or visual
    identity?

Do not ask every question in one overwhelming questionnaire. Gather
essential information conversationally and make assumptions explicit.

### Phase 2 --- Finalize the brand name

-   Evaluate the candidate names in this document.
-   Generate additional candidates only if they improve the shortlist.
-   Check pronunciation, spelling, memorability, and relevance.
-   Check domain availability using reliable current sources.
-   Check relevant social media handle availability where practical.
-   Search for potential trademark conflicts in the relevant markets and
    classes.
-   Clearly distinguish a preliminary search from legal trademark
    clearance.
-   Present a concise shortlist with reasons and tagline options.
-   Obtain the user's explicit approval before treating a name as final.

### Phase 3 --- Brand identity

After the name is approved:

-   Develop the brand positioning and value proposition.
-   Refine the tagline.
-   Propose a visual direction, including colors, typography, logo
    concept, and imagery.
-   Ensure the identity works across desktop, mobile, app icons,
    packaging (if relevant), and social media.
-   Keep the identity consistent and accessible.

### Phase 4 --- Business and product planning

Based on the confirmed business model, define:

-   Customer journey and key user flows.
-   Required website/app pages.
-   Product or service discovery and detail experiences.
-   Cart, checkout, payments, and order handling if applicable.
-   Customer accounts and support.
-   Admin operations and reporting.
-   Vendor/partner capabilities only if the business model requires
    them.
-   Security, privacy, reliability, and scalability requirements.

Do not build features merely because they are common in e-commerce. Tie
every feature to the confirmed business model and user needs.

### Phase 5 --- Technical design and implementation

Once scope is confirmed:

-   Recommend an appropriate architecture and technology stack.
-   Define the data model and API boundaries.
-   Plan authentication, authorization, payments, notifications, and
    integrations as applicable.
-   Establish coding standards, testing strategy, deployment approach,
    and operational monitoring.
-   Build incrementally, validating each milestone with the user.
-   Keep documentation updated as decisions are made.
-   Do not claim that a feature, integration, domain, or deployment is
    complete unless it has actually been implemented and verified.

## 7. Working Rules for the Agent

1.  Treat this document as the record of the initial brainstorming
    conversation.
2.  The user has not yet selected a final business name.
3.  The user has not yet confirmed the exact business model or product
    category.
4.  Do not silently invent business requirements.
5.  Ask only the most important next question when clarification is
    needed.
6.  Explain recommendations in plain language and give practical
    reasons.
7.  Prefer a maintainable, production-minded solution over unnecessary
    complexity.
8.  Keep a record of confirmed decisions, open questions, assumptions,
    and completed work.
9.  Validate domains, trademarks, integrations, and other changing facts
    with current sources.
10. Get approval before making irreversible or externally visible
    changes, purchasing services, registering domains, or publishing the
    brand.

## 8. Immediate Next Step

Start by confirming what kind of online business the user intends to
build. Then help narrow the name shortlist and validate the strongest
candidates.

Do not begin full website or application implementation until the
business model, target customer, and initial scope are sufficiently
clear.
